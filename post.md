All five crvUSD PegKeepers have had nothing put into their pools since 23:49 UTC on 29 October, so they can do nothing while crvUSD trades below 1.

What a PegKeeper does

Each can hold crvUSD given to it by Curve. It may put some into its pool only when the price Curve uses is at or above 1, and take some back out only when it is at or below 1. It can only take out what it put in. That amount is its debt.

Where they stand now

All five have had no debt since 23:49 UTC on 29 October. Four still hold crvUSD they could put in. GHO's ceiling is zero.

Why the largest one sat still

The USDT PegKeeper held almost all the debt, about 34M crvUSD. It stayed flat for twelve days, and for about ten of them the price was below 1. In about nine in ten hourly readings over those days its pool held less crvUSD than the other coin, so the contract would try to deposit and be refused.

What it would take to act again

The price must be at or above 1 and a pool must be short of crvUSD. The price was at or above 1 in about 7% of hourly readings, and none in November came within 0.01% of 1. The pools now hold 72% to 84% crvUSD, so between 3M and 8M crvUSD would have to leave each of four of them.

Limits

Readings are hourly. Deposit blocks come from the contract rule and pool balances. Withdrawal profitability comes from simulation at one block per hour. Nearly all the evidence is one PegKeeper over 77 days of falling prices. The borrow rate was not studied.
